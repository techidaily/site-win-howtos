### Fix 1: Log in and out of your Google account

 This is a quick fix for you when there are no other solutions in sight. Simply log out of your Google account (supposing you have already logged in) and then log back in. If it works for you, that probably suggests you’re just having a hiccup in Chrome. No big deal.

If you are not sure how to sign out of your account, here are the steps:

 1) Click your**profile** at the top right corner of your Chrome browser. Then select**Sync is on** .

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-syn-off1.jpg)

 2) Click the**Turn off** button.

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-syn-off2.jpg)

Then confirm in the pop-up message window.

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-syn-off3.jpg)

### Fix 3: Check your “Passwords” settings

 Sometimes the incorrect “Passwords” settings of Chrome can be the culprit for your trouble. Maybe you have once disabled the auto-filling feature but forgot to enable it again. In this case, you should check the related settings in your browser and see if there’s any problem within.

 1) Click the**three-stacked-dots icon** at the top right corner of your Chrome browser. Place your cursor over**Passwords and autofill** and then select**Google Password Manager** from the menu.

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-check-password1.jpg)

 2) Select the**Settings** tab. Then make sure you turn on these two toggles:

* **Offer to save passwords**
* **Sign in** **automatically** .

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-check-password2-1200x380.jpg)

 3) Scroll down the page and take a look at the**Declined sites and apps** section. If you notice any sites banned from saving your passwords – which is not what you want – simply remove it from the list by clicking the**Cancel** icon.

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-check-password3.jpg)

 Unable to find anything abnormal in your settings? Don’t worry, there’re still other fixes awaiting your try.

### Fix 4: Clear cache and cookies

 At the sight of Fix 3, you may wonder why clearing cache and cookies can solve this problem. Sounds a bit weird, but it does have something to do with your passwords-not-saving issue – this is possibly because the overload of cache has interfered with the normal functioning of your Chrome password manager.

 Incidentally, if you cannot open any pages via Google Chrome with a notification that reads “this site can’t be reached,” you can also try to clear up your browsing data and see whether that helps you regain access to websites. But this is not our focal point here, so let’s just turn back to the original topic.

 To clear the cache and cookies stored in Chrome, you may perform the following procedure:

1) Open your Chrome browser, and press Ctrl + Shift + Del.

2) In the pop-up window, you’ll see three options under the Basic tab:

* **Browsing history**
* **Cookies and other site data**
* **Cached images and files** .

 Make certain you check them all. Then, click**Clear data** .

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-Clear-cache.jpg)

### **Fix 5: Remove unwanted programs**

 The Chrome Cleanup Tool for**Chrome 111** or later has been removed, so you can skip this fix to the[next one](#fix5) if your Chrome has been updated to the version above 111.

 To rule out the possibility that some malware has prevented Chrome from saving your passwords, you can rely on Chrome’s built-in component to remove suspicious or unwanted programs (ones that may cause issues with the browser) for you.

 1) Click the**three-stacked-dots icon** at the top right corner of your Chrome browser. Then select**Settings** .

![This image has an empty alt attribute; its file name is image-525.png](https://images.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/image-525.png)

 2) On the pop-up page, scroll down to the bottom and click**Advanced** .

![](https://images.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/image-342.png)

 3) Under**Reset and clean up** , click**Clean up computer** .

![](https://images.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/image-560.png)

### **Fix 6: Reset the browser to default settings**

 It’s worth trying to reset your Google Chrome to its original defaults. Before you perform the following procedure, don’t forget to back up your browser settings in case you lose any data during the restoration process.

 If you’re not sure how to back up the settings and import them later, you should check out **[this post](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/96816?hl=en)**  from Google Support.

 To restore your Chrome settings to its defaults, take the following steps:

 1) Click the**three-stacked-dots icon** at the top right corner of your Chrome browser. Then select**Settings** .

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-setting-reset.jpg)

 2) Select**Reset settings** and then click**Restore settings to their original defaults** on the right.

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-reset2-1200x636.jpg)

 3) Click**Reset settings** .

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-reset3.jpg)

 Once your settings have been restored, check to see whether your problem remains. If it does, try out the next method.

### **Fix 7: Check for Chrome updates**

 Google Chrome will search for the latest update and install it automatically in most cases, though, it may fail to do so at times and thus give rise to unknown errors. Try to update your Chrome manually and see whether you can get through the problem by this way.

 1) Click the**three-stacked-dots icon** at the top right corner of your Chrome browser. Then select**Help > About Google Chrome** .

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-about.jpg)

 2) On the next page, you’ll see the version of you Chrome. The browser will check for available updates instantly and install them by itself.

![](https://www.drivereasy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/chrome-version.jpg)
